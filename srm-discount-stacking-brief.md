# Discount stacking — SRM briefing

Tex Dex B2B · Internal · Go-live **Monday, November 9, 2026**

Growth@ will tell suppliers discounts are changing. That email cannot carry the relationship. Make the change feel like a simplification they control, not a Q4 cost surprise.

## Role of the SRM

You own the relationship, the number, and the paper trail. Growth@ is notice. You are the person who makes the math land as intended.

**Sound.** Calm, specific, on their side of the spreadsheet. Not selling a program. Not apologizing for a policy. If you do not know an edge case, say you will confirm in Slack. Do not invent stacking rules in the room.

**Say.** Intended discount · what Pro customers have actually been receiving · you control this in Pricing Home · confirm before November 9 · stay in line with today’s investment · simpler to maintain.

**Never say.** Erosion · we are stacking you deeper · $13M · GRS · egregious · you have been under-investing · “this is required.” Never bargain with B2C. Everyday B2B is the lever. Event depth is not.

### What actually changes

Today only the deepest discount applies. A 10% B2C promo wipes a 5% Everyday B2B discount. The Pro customer pays the B2C price, the Pro Price flag comes off, and suppliers rebuild B2B by hand in every source room.

November 9 is a **backend calculation only**. Not a new tool.

- Live B2C promo applies first (major shopping holidays and short-term promos).
- Everyday B2B then applies **on top of that promotional cost**.
- Flash, Doorbusters, and Deals of the Day also apply on top of the live B2C promo.
- Everyday B2B and item-level deals **do not** stack with each other.
- Event, weekend, superroom, and extended discount rooms still **do not** stack with each other.
- The B2B column in promo rooms is going away. Leftover B2B promo inputs are ignored on November 9. Pro pricing has to live as Everyday B2B in Pricing Home. Changes take effect immediately. PG/SRB Trade Discount does not change.

The only example you need: **$100 base → 10% B2C promo = $90 → 5% Everyday B2B off $90 = $85.50 Pro cost.** Say “off the promo cost” out loud.

The trap they will invent: 10% event + 5% Flash + 5% B2B ≠ 20%. Flash stacks on the event, not also on Everyday B2B. If Flash matches or beats Everyday B2B, prices can tie and the Pro Price flag disappears. Expected.

### Your job this cycle

Pull the data sheet. For every SU write down **[X] = Everyday B2B on file** and **[Y] = L90D effective**, plus bucket.

Call order: stacked result >20% or +10 points vs effective first (so November 9 does not create a surprise PO) → full Everyday-on-paper / ~0% during events → partial → limited (light confirm) → zero Everyday B2B (FYI).

The ask, in this order, every time: keep current Everyday B2B → move to last-90-day effective (investment-neutral) → land above 5% → 5% or 0% if they escalate → **never pull B2C to fund B2B**.

This week: mock pitch a hard supplier with your assigned L3+ (NA 9/24–10/2). After 9/28 Growth@, send the short email and book the call soon after. Push October 13 webinar participation.

On the call: frame → $85.50 → [X] vs [Y] → land a number → close back doors (B2C stays whole; new parts still need Everyday B2B in PA 2.0; Flash may hide the Pro flag) → recap the same day.

Before November 9, the input is updated **or** they have explicitly chosen to keep [X]. Document it. Week of go-live, spot-check hero SKUs.

Use with data-oriented partners: Pro Price filter is #1 on the B2B site; 13% of clickers convert in-session; SKUs that kept B2B through Way Day 2025 grew +2,100 bps vs those that did not.

## Talk tracks

If they already got Growth@, do not re-read the email. Move to the math.

### Open → change → example → limits → catalog → close

**Open.**  
“I wanted this on our next call so you are not first seeing it in Partner Home during Cyber. On Monday, November 9, we are changing how discounts calculate. It is simpler, and you still set the percent. It is not a new ask for extra points.”

**The change.**  
“Today, only the deepest discount applies. So if you have 5% Everyday B2B and we run a 10% event, the 5% disappears and a Pro customer pays the same as everyone else. Starting November 9, Everyday B2B applies on top of the live promotional cost. You set it once in Pricing Home. You do not rebuild it in every source room.”

**The example.**  
“Easy numbers: $100 base cost. 10% Cyber on the consumer side is $90. Your 5% Everyday B2B comes off that $90, not off $100. Pro cost is $85.50. You still have a real Pro price during the event, without loading a second B2B number into the Cyber room.”

**Name the limits before they invent them.**  
“Two important limits. One: Flash, Doorbusters, and Everyday B2B do not all stack together. Flash sits on the event. Everyday B2B sits on the event. They do not sit on each other. Two: extended rooms, weekend rooms, and superrooms still do not stack with the holiday room. There is no workaround of parking B2B in an extended room.”

**Their catalog.**  
“On your assortment, Everyday B2B on file is [X]%. Over the last 90 days, what Pro customers have actually been receiving is closer to [Y]%. On November 9, [X]% starts to invoice during events unless we change the input. If [X]% is what you intend for Pro, we should keep it — that is the best outcome for your Pro business, and it is much less work. If [X]% was never meant to hold through Cyber, we should set Everyday B2B to [Y]% so your investment stays in line with today.”  
→ “Does [X]% still represent what you want to give, or is [Y]% the more honest number?”

**Close.**  
“You can change this anytime in Pricing Home and it goes live immediately. I would rather we choose the number in October than discover it on a Cyber PO. I will send a three-line recap today: the November 9 date, the number we landed, and where to edit it. There is a supplier webinar Tuesday, October 13 at 11am if finance wants to hear it from the pricing team.”

### Same-day recap (three lines)

“As discussed: starting Monday, November 9, Everyday B2B will apply on top of the live promotional cost. We agreed to [keep X% / update Everyday B2B to Y% in Pricing Home before November 9]. Happy to chat through these changes on our next call if finance has questions after they read this.”

They go silent after Growth@: do not assume they read it. Short email, then a calendar hold: “15 min — November 9 discount change.” High-gap or full-event wipe no-show: call.

### Words that keep the room calm

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| Your discount has been eroding | What Pro customers have actually been receiving is [Y]% |
| We need you to maintain investment | If [X]% is what you intend, keep it; if not, [Y]% keeps you in line with today |
| Stacking will make you deeper | The percent in Pricing Home will finally be the percent that invoices during events |
| You have to opt in | This happens November 9 for everyone; you control the percent |
| Egregious / risk flag | I want to look at this with you before go-live so the PO matches what you meant |
| Margin-neutral | Same overall B2B investment you have been giving, just a cleaner input |
| Don’t pull B2C (abrupt) | Event depth and Pro depth are different decisions; we change Everyday B2B, not the holiday |

### Bucket openers

**On-file far from what has been invoicing.**  
“I flagged this one because the percent on file and the percent that has been invoicing are far apart. If we do nothing, November 9 could invoice a much deeper Pro cost than you have been running. I would rather we set the input together than have a PO conversation in December.”

**Everyday on paper, Pro in line with B2C during events.**  
“You have [X]% Everyday B2B in the tool, and during major events Pro has effectively been in line with B2C. Stacking means [X]% will start to show up in those events. That can be a win for Pro if it is intended. If it is not, we should move the input to [Y]% now.”

**Protects B2B some of the time, not others.**  
“You already protect B2B some of the time and not others. After November 9 you will not have to babysit rooms to keep Pro alive. The only decision is whether [X]% is the rate you want live all the time.”

**Already keeping Pro through events by hand.**  
“This should make your life easier. You have been doing the manual work to keep Pro through events. Now the system does that. No action if you are comfortable with [X]%. I still wanted you to hear it from me.”

**No Everyday B2B.**  
“There is no cost change for you on November 9. If you add Everyday B2B later, you will not have to rebuild it for every event. Happy to look at a 5% line if and when it is useful. Nothing you need to do this week.”

### Difficult conversations

**“This is a margin grab.”** They heard “stacking” as extra points. Separate the engine from the input.  
“I understand why it sounds that way. Today [X]% is not what has been invoicing during events. Effective has been about [Y]%. On November 9 the system will honor whatever is in Pricing Home — so if we leave [X]%, yes, Cyber Pro cost goes to [X]% on top of the event. That is only a deepen if [X]% was theater. If your intent was never [X]% through Cyber, we change the input to [Y]% and your investment stays where it has been. What I cannot do is leave [X]% in the tool and also promise it will keep getting wiped.”  
Face-saving line: “This is correcting a stale setting, not agreeing to a new rate.”  
Do not say everyone else is keeping 5%. Do not say it’s only a few points.

**“We’ll take points out of B2C to pay for B2B.”** Red line. Same sentence. Do not take it away to think.  
“I need to be direct on that one. We cannot fund Everyday B2B by thinning the event or Flash. Consumer events still have to stand on their own. Pro is an additional layer. If the stacked Pro cost is too rich, we change Everyday B2B — keep [X]%, move to [Y]%, or go lower. We do not take points out of Way Day or Cyber to make the Pro math work.”  
If they push: “I can be flexible on the Pro percent. I cannot be flexible on using B2C as the budget. If we need a broader conversation, I will bring my lead in — we still will not trade event depth for this.” Escalate the same day.

**“Fine. Set it to zero.”** Business choice, not a tantrum.  
“You can do that, and it takes effect immediately. I want to be clear what you are turning off: the Pro Price flag, the Pro Price filter — which is the top filter on the Pro site — and the conversion those flags drive. There is no source-room workaround during Cyber once Everyday B2B is zero. If zero is a reaction to stacking and not a P&L decision, can we park at [Y]% through Q4 and look again in January after you have seen net cost in Partner Home?”  
If they hold: “Understood. I will log Everyday B2B at 0% as your decision, and we will not touch your B2C submissions because of it. I will come back after Cyber so you can see the Pro merchandising impact with a clean read.” Do not punish Flash or holiday in the same meeting.

**“Our CFO will never approve a deeper Cyber cost.”** They need a packet, not a better speech.  
“They should not have to approve going deeper. They have to approve that the number in Pricing Home matches intent. I will send three lines they can react to: one, go-live is November 9, calculation only; two, $100 becomes $90 on a 10% event, then 5% Pro off $90 is $85.50; three, our recommendation is [Y]% if the goal is to stay at the investment you have actually been giving. Happy to do 15 minutes with them on the line. Waiting without changing the input is a decision to keep [X]%.”

**“Nobody told us. This is last minute.”**  
“Fair that you want this from a person, not only an email. The site change is November 9, not this week. Growth@ is the formal notice, the webinar is October 13, and Partner Home will show the stack in early October so you can see it before it bills. That is why I wanted it on this call — so you can choose the input with time to spare, including leaving it alone.”

**“We’ll keep putting B2B in the promo spreadsheet.”**  
“That column is going away. On November 9 the system ignores B2B promo inputs. If Pro pricing is not in Everyday B2B, it will not be on the site. Loading it in the Cyber room will not save it.”

**“What about Flash?”**  
“Flash becomes incremental to the live event cost — that is why you submit it. It still does not replace a real event cost, and it still does not stack with Everyday B2B. If an item is on Flash, the Pro customer gets Flash, not Flash plus Pro. We expect more SKUs to clear Flash rules. The per-SKU Flash fee still applies. I do not want stacking anxiety to knock you out of Flash.”

**“Will we still show as Pro priced during Cyber?”**  
“When Everyday B2B makes Pro 5% cheaper than B2C, yes — flag, filter, the full merchandising. When a Flash Deal matches or beats Everyday B2B, the prices can be the same and the flag will not show on that SKU. The flag means differentiated Pro price, not ‘this item exists on the Pro site.’”

**“We’ll wait and see after Cyber.”**  
“The engine will not wait. Whatever is in Pricing Home on November 9 is what stacks. Waiting is choosing to keep [X]%. If that is not intended, we should change it this month.”

**“Can you just do it for us?”**  
They update Pricing Home (or you do, if that is already how you support them). Do not promise a central move that does not exist.
