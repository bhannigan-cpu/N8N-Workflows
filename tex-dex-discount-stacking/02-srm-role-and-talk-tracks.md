# Discount stacking — SRM executive briefing

**Tex Dex B2B · Internal · Go-live Monday, November 9, 2026**

Growth@ will tell suppliers that discounts are changing. That email cannot carry the relationship. Your job is to make the change feel like a simplification they control, not a Q4 cost surprise.

This briefing is the language to use. Read it once. Take the scripts onto the call. Do not forward it.

---

## How we sound

You are calm, specific, and on their side of the spreadsheet. You are not selling a program and you are not apologizing for a policy.

**Say:** intended discount · what Pro customers have actually been receiving · you control this in Pricing Home · confirm before November 9 · stay in line with today’s investment · simpler to maintain

**Do not say:** erosion · we are stacking you deeper · $13M · GRS · egregious · you have been under-investing · deepest discount wins (as a lecture) · “this is required”

**Never bargain with B2C.** If they offer to thin Way Day, Cyber, or Flash to “pay for” Pro, shut that down in the same sentence. Everyday B2B is the lever. Event depth is not.

If you do not know the answer, say you will confirm in `#temp-stacking-questions-26`. Inventing stacking rules in the room is how we get PO fights in December.

---

## 1. What you need to know

Today only the deepest discount applies. A 10% B2C promo wipes a 5% Everyday B2B discount. The Pro customer pays the B2C price, the Pro Price flag comes off, and suppliers rebuild B2B by hand in every source room.

On November 9 that math changes. It is a backend calculation, not a new tool.

- Live B2C promo applies first (major shopping holidays and short-term promos).
- Everyday B2B then applies on top of that promotional cost.
- Flash Deals, Doorbusters, and Deals of the Day also apply on top of the live B2C promo.
- Everyday B2B and item-level deals **do not stack with each other**. The customer gets the deeper of those two.
- Event, weekend, superroom, and **extended discount rooms still do not stack with each other**.

**The only example you need:** $100 base cost. 10% B2C promo → $90. 5% Everyday B2B is taken off **$90**, not $100. B2B cost = **$85.50**. Say “off the promo cost” out loud. If you round to $85, they will think you are hiding a point.

**The trap they will invent:** 10% event + 5% Flash + 5% B2B ≠ 20%. Flash stacks on the event. It does not also stack on Everyday B2B. If Flash matches or beats Everyday B2B, B2C and Pro can be the same price and the Pro Price flag disappears. That is expected, not a bug. Flash is excluded from Everyday B2B stacking so a short B2C push is not taxed three layers deep.

The B2B column in promo rooms is going away. Any leftover B2B *promo* input is ignored on November 9. If they want Pro pricing, it has to live as Everyday B2B in Pricing Home. They can change that percent anytime; it takes effect immediately.

PG/SRB Trade Discount is a separate program. It does not change.

**Why Pro Pricing is still worth defending (use with data-oriented partners, not as a quota speech):** the Pro Price filter is #1 on the B2B site; 13% of customers who click it convert in the same session; SKUs that kept B2B discounts through Way Day 2025 grew +2,100 bps versus those that did not. Do not cite the internal $13M Q4 forecast.

**The ask, in order, every time:**
1. Keep current Everyday B2B.
2. If they cannot, move to last-90-day effective — what they have actually been giving — so they stay investment-neutral.
3. If they cannot hold that, land above 5%.
4. If they escalate, 5% or 0%.
5. Never take points out of B2C to fund B2B.

[X] = Everyday B2B on file. [Y] = L90D effective. Write both down before you dial.

## 3. Talk tracks and language

### The call, in order

**Open — put them at ease, then name the date.**

“I wanted this on our next call so you are not first seeing it in Partner Home during Cyber. On Monday, November 9, we are changing how discounts calculate. It is simpler, and you still set the percent. It is not a new ask for extra points.”

Stop. Let them react. If they say they already got Growth@, do not re-read the email. Move to the math.

**Explain the change — one breath.**

“Today, only the deepest discount applies. So if you have 5% Everyday B2B and we run a 10% event, the 5% disappears and a Pro customer pays the same as everyone else. Starting November 9, Everyday B2B applies on top of the live promotional cost. You set it once in Pricing Home. You do not rebuild it in every source room.”

**The example — always this one.**

“Easy numbers: $100 base cost. 10% Cyber on the consumer side is $90. Your 5% Everyday B2B comes off that $90, not off $100. Pro cost is $85.50. You still have a real Pro price during the event, without loading a second B2B number into the Cyber room.”

**Name what does *not* change, before they invent it.**

“Two important limits, so we are not surprised later. One: Flash, Doorbusters, and Everyday B2B do not all stack together. Flash sits on the event. Everyday B2B sits on the event. They do not sit on each other. Two: extended rooms, weekend rooms, and superrooms still do not stack with the holiday room. There is no workaround of parking B2B in an extended room.”

**Bring it to their catalog.**

“On your assortment, Everyday B2B on file is [X]%. Over the last 90 days, what Pro customers have actually been receiving is closer to [Y]%. On November 9, [X]% starts to invoice during events unless we change the input. If [X]% is what you intend for Pro, we should keep it — that is the best outcome for your Pro business, and it is much less work. If [X]% was never meant to hold through Cyber, we should set Everyday B2B to [Y]% so your investment stays in line with today.”

Then ask, and wait: “Does [X]% still represent what you want to give, or is [Y]% the more honest number?”

**Close the decision.**

“You can change this anytime in Pricing Home and it goes live immediately. I would rather we choose the number in October than discover it on a Cyber PO. I will send a three-line recap today: the November 9 date, the number we landed, and where to edit it. There is also a supplier webinar Tuesday, October 13 at 11am if finance wants to hear it from the pricing team. Happy to walk through any of this again on our next call once they have looked at it.”

### Bucket openers (first 30 seconds after the frame)

**Egregious / very high Everyday versus very low effective.**  
“I flagged this one because the percent on file and the percent that has been invoicing are far apart. If we do nothing, November 9 could invoice a much deeper Pro cost than you have been running. I would rather we set the input together than have a PO conversation in December.”

**Full erosion (they let events wipe B2B).**  
“You have [X]% Everyday B2B in the tool, and during major events Pro has effectively been in line with B2C. Stacking means [X]% will start to show up in those events. That can be a win for Pro if it is intended. If it is not, we should move the input to [Y]% now.”

**Partial erosion.**  
“You already protect B2B some of the time and not others. After November 9 you will not have to babysit rooms to keep Pro alive. The only decision is whether [X]% is the rate you want live all the time.”

**Limited erosion.**  
“This should make your life easier. You have been doing the manual work to keep Pro through events. Now the system does that. No action if you are comfortable with [X]%. I still wanted you to hear it from me.”

**No Everyday B2B.**  
“There is no cost change for you on November 9. If you add Everyday B2B later, you will not have to rebuild it for every event. Happy to look at a 5% line if and when it is useful. Nothing you need to do this week.”

**Perigold sensitive.**  
“This is separate from Trade Discount, which is not changing. Because your Everyday rate and what has actually invoiced during events are quite different, we can move you to [Y]% on your behalf so you stay in line with today, including below the usual 5% floor if needed. That happens unless you tell us not to by October 19. You can also keep [X]% if that is truly the intent.”

### Difficult conversations

Each block is what they mean, the language to use, and the line you do not cross.

---

**“This is a margin grab. You are forcing us deeper on Cyber.”**

They heard “stacking” as “extra points.” Do not argue intent. Separate the engine from the input.

“I understand why it sounds that way. Today [X]% is not what has been invoicing during events. Effective has been about [Y]%. On November 9 the system will honor whatever is in Pricing Home — so if we leave [X]%, yes, Cyber Pro cost goes to [X]% on top of the event. That is only a deepen if [X]% was theater. If your intent was never [X]% through Cyber, we change the input to [Y]% and your investment stays where it has been. What I cannot do is leave [X]% in the tool and also promise it will keep getting wiped.”

If they need a face-saving win: “This is correcting a stale setting, not agreeing to a new rate.”

Do not say: “Everyone else is keeping 5%.” Do not say: “It’s only a few points.” Do not cite $13M.

---

**“We’ll take 5 points out of B2C to pay for B2B.”**

This is the only red line. Firm in the same sentence. Do not take it away to think about.

“I need to be direct on that one. We cannot fund Everyday B2B by thinning the event or Flash. Consumer events still have to stand on their own. Pro is an additional layer. If the stacked Pro cost is too rich, we change Everyday B2B — keep [X]%, move to [Y]%, or go lower. We do not take points out of Way Day or Cyber to make the Pro math work.”

If they push: “I can be flexible on the Pro percent. I cannot be flexible on using B2C as the budget. If we need a broader conversation, I will bring my lead in — we still will not trade event depth for this.”

Then escalate the same day. Do not leave “we’ll revisit B2C” as an open item.

---

**“Fine. Set it to zero.”**

Treat it as a business choice, not a tantrum, then put the cost on the table without threatening.

“You can do that, and it takes effect immediately. I want to be clear what you are turning off: the Pro Price flag, the Pro Price filter — which is the top filter on the Pro site — and the conversion those flags drive. There is no source-room workaround during Cyber once Everyday B2B is zero. If zero is a reaction to stacking and not a P&L decision, can we park at [Y]% through Q4 and look again in January after you have seen net cost in Partner Home?”

If they hold at zero: “Understood. I will log Everyday B2B at 0% as your decision, and we will not touch your B2C submissions because of it. I will come back after Cyber so you can see the Pro merchandising impact with a clean read.”

Do not punish Flash or holiday participation in the same meeting.

---

**“We already bleed on Wayfair. We already give Trade.”**

Common on design-forward Tex Dex and anything touching Perigold.

“Trade Discount on Perigold and SRB is a different program, and it is not changing. I am not stacking a new Trade ask on this. This conversation is only whether the Everyday B2B number in Pricing Home should stay at [X]% or move to [Y]% so November 9 does not accidentally increase the Pro investment you already feel.”

---

**“Our CFO will never approve a deeper Cyber cost.”**

They need a packet, not a better speech.

“They should not have to approve going deeper. They have to approve that the number in Pricing Home matches intent. I will send three lines they can react to: one, go-live is November 9, calculation only; two, $100 becomes $90 on a 10% event, then 5% Pro off $90 is $85.50; three, our recommendation is [Y]% if the goal is to stay at the investment you have actually been giving. Happy to do 15 minutes with them on the line. Waiting without changing the input is a decision to keep [X]%.”

---

**“Nobody told us. This is last minute.”**

Do not get defensive about the timeline. Give them time that is still real.

“Fair that you want this from a person, not only an email. The site change is November 9, not this week. Growth@ is the formal notice, the webinar is October 13, and Partner Home will show the stack in early October so you can see it before it bills. That is why I wanted it on this call — so you can choose the input with time to spare, including leaving it alone.”

Then book the follow-up on the calendar before you hang up. “Happy to chat through these changes on our next call” only works if there is a next call.

---

**“Your 6% average is not our assortment.”**

Agree. Averages hide hero SKUs.

“Let’s not die on a catalog average. We can open this at part level. If the core of the business is one rate and a tail is another, we set line-wide to the honest core rather than ship a blended [X]% into stacking. Getting a clean number on the SKUs that matter is the win.”

---

**“We’ll keep putting B2B in the promo spreadsheet like we always do.”**

“That column is going away. On November 9 the system ignores B2B promo inputs. If Pro pricing is not in Everyday B2B, it will not be on the site. Loading it in the Cyber room will not save it.”

---

**“What about Flash this week?”**

“Flash becomes incremental to the live event cost — that is why you submit it. It still does not replace a real event cost, and it still does not stack with Everyday B2B. If an item is on Flash, the Pro customer gets Flash, not Flash plus Pro. We expect more SKUs to clear Flash rules. The per-SKU Flash fee still applies. I do not want stacking anxiety to knock you out of Flash.”

---

**“Will we still show as Pro priced during Cyber?”**

“When Everyday B2B makes Pro cheaper than B2C, yes — flag, filter, the full merchandising. When a Flash Deal matches or beats Everyday B2B, the prices can be the same and the flag will not show on that SKU. The flag means differentiated Pro price, not ‘this item exists on the Pro site.’”

---

**“We’ll wait and see after Cyber.”**

“The engine will not wait. Whatever is in Pricing Home on November 9 is what stacks. Waiting is choosing to keep [X]%. If that is not intended, we should change it this month.”

---

**“Can you just do it for us?”**

Perigold sensitive: yes, central process, unless they opt out by October 19. Everyone else: they update Everyday B2B in Pricing Home (or you do, if that is already how you support them). Do not promise a central move that does not exist.

---

**They go silent after Growth@.**

Do not assume they read it. Send the short email, then a calendar hold: “15 min — November 9 discount change.” If they no-show and they are egregious or full erosion, call.
