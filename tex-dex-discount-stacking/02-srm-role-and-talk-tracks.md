# SRM role: Discount stacking (Tex Dex)

**Go-live: Monday, November 9, 2026**  
Internal only. Growth@ announces the change. You make sure suppliers are not blindsided and that the Everyday B2B percent in Pricing Home is the percent they actually mean to give.

---

## What you need to know

**Today:** Deepest discount wins. A 10% B2C promo wipes a 5% Everyday B2B discount. Pro customers pay the same as B2C, lose the Pro Price flag, and suppliers rebuild B2B by hand in every source room.

**Starting November 9:** Backend math change only. Tools stay the same.

1. Live B2C promo applies first (major shopping holidays and short-term promos).
2. Everyday B2B then applies **on top of that promo cost**.
3. Flash / Doorbusters / DOTD also apply on top of the live B2C promo.
4. Everyday B2B and item-level deals **do not stack with each other**. The customer gets the deeper of those two, not both.
5. B2C rooms still do **not** stack with each other (events, weekend rooms, superrooms, **extended discount rooms**).

**The math:** $100 base → 10% B2C promo = $90 → 5% Everyday B2B **off $90** = **$85.50**. Not $85. Not 15% off $100.

**The trap:** 10% event + 5% Flash + 5% B2B ≠ 20%. Flash stacks on the event. It does not also stack on Everyday B2B. If Flash matches or beats B2B, B2C and B2B can be the same price and the **Pro Price flag disappears**. That is expected.

**B2B promo rooms go away.** Leftover B2B promo inputs are ignored on 11/9. If they want Pro pricing, it has to live as Everyday B2B in Pricing Home. Changes still take effect immediately.

**PG/SRB Trade Discount does not change.** Do not mix that program into this conversation.

**Why it matters:** Pro Price filter is #1 on the B2B site. SKUs that kept B2B discounts through Way Day 2025 grew +2,100 bps vs those that did not. Do not cite the $13M Q4 forecast to suppliers.

**Negotiation ladder (in this order):** keep current Everyday B2B → move to L90D effective (margin-neutral) → land above 5% if you can → 5% or 0% if they escalate → **never** pull B2C depth to fund B2B.

2. **Prioritize in this order**

   - **Full erosion (required):** Everyday B2B on paper, ~0% during major promos. Stacking turns the paper % into a real %.
   - **Partial erosion (recommended):** follow up on Growth@; keep current or move to effective.
   - **Limited erosion (optional):** confirm they saw the note; this mostly makes their life easier.
   - **No Everyday B2B:** no cost change on 11/9. FYI only; pitch adding B2B later if the relationship can take it.
   - **Perigold sensitive:** audit the list; opt-outs due **10/19**. Central team can waive the 5% minimum and move them to effective unless they opt out.

6. **Before 11/9:** Everyday B2B is updated **or** they have explicitly chosen to keep current depth. Document it.

7. **Week of 11/9:** Spot-check a few hero SKUs (B2C cost, B2B cost, Pro flag, any Flash).

## Talk tracks

### Open
“Starting November 9, Everyday B2B will apply on top of the live promotional cost instead of getting erased by it. You still control the percent in Pricing Home, and you can change it anytime. I am not asking you to go deeper than you already planned. I am asking you to confirm the percent sitting there is the percent you actually want during Q4 events.”

### Why this is easier
“Today, keeping a 5% Pro price means re-entering it in every event room, Flash, closeout, and extended room. After November 9 you set Everyday B2B once and add it on new parts. That’s it.”

### Their numbers
“Your Everyday input is [X]%. What Pro customers have actually been getting over the last 90 days is [Y]%. On November 9, [X]% will start landing during events unless we change the input. Ideal is keep [X]%. If that is too rich, [Y]% keeps you margin-neutral.”

### Flash
“Flash stacks on the event. It does not also stack on Everyday B2B. Pro customers do not get all three. If a SKU is on Flash, they get the Flash path. The Pro Price flag only shows when Pro is actually cheaper than B2C.”

### “This is a margin grab.”
“I get why it looks like that. Today [X]% is not what we invoice during events — effective has been about [Y]%. On November 9 the engine will honor [X]% unless we change the input. If you never meant to give [X]% during Cyber, we set Everyday B2B to [Y]% and you stay flat. What I cannot do is leave [X]% in the tool and also promise it keeps getting wiped.”

### “We’ll pull B2C to fund B2B.”
“That is not an option we can support. Event and Flash depth have to stand on their own. If stacked B2B is too rich, we change Everyday B2B — keep current, move to your effective rate, or go lower. We do not thin holiday or Flash to make the Pro math work.”

### “We’ll set Everyday B2B to zero.”
“You can, and it takes effect immediately. That turns off the Pro Price flag and filter on those SKUs. If zero is a protest and not a P&L decision, can we park at [Y]% through Q4 and revisit in January once you’ve seen net cost in Partner Home?” If they still go to zero: thank them, log it, do not retaliate on B2C.

### “We’ll keep loading B2B in the promo spreadsheet.”
“That column is going away. On November 9 the system ignores B2B promo inputs. If you want Pro pricing, it has to live as Everyday B2B.”

### “Nobody told us / we need to wait for our CFO.”
“The site change is November 9, not this week. Growth@ is the formal notice, the webinar is October 13, and Partner Home will show the stack in early October. I’ll send three lines they can react to: go-live date, the $100 → $90 → $85.50 math, and recommended input [Y]% to stay at your last-90-day investment. Waiting is a decision to keep [X]%.”

### “Your catalog average is wrong.”
“Let’s look at part-level in Negotiation Hub. If the core of the assortment is one rate and a tail is another, we should set line-wide to the honest core rather than ship a blended number into stacking.”

### “We already give Trade.”
“PG/SRB Trade Discount is a different program and is not changing. This is only about whether Everyday B2B in Pricing Home should stay at [X]% or move to [Y]% so stacking does not accidentally increase the investment you already feel.”
