## 2. The change, said accurately

Use this as your internal source of truth. Gemini meeting notes are directionally useful and **not** always precise. Corrections are in the appendix.

### Today (through 11/8)

Deepest discount wins. A 10% B2C promo can fully erase a 5% Everyday B2B discount. The Pro customer pays the same as B2C and loses the Pro Price flag. Suppliers who want a true 5% B2B line have to rebuild it in every source room, including major events, Flash, Doorbusters, DOTD, closeouts, extended rooms, and aisle promos.

### Starting 11/9

This is a **backend calculation change**, not a new supplier tool.

1. Start from item base cost.
2. Apply the live B2C promotional cost (major shopping holidays and short-term promo rooms). B2C rooms still **do not stack with each other** — events, weekend rooms, superrooms, and extended discount rooms remain deepest-wins among themselves.
3. Then:
   - **Everyday B2B** stacks on that promotional cost, **or**
   - **Item-level deals** (Flash, Doorbusters; official wrap-up also includes Deals of the Day) stack on that promotional cost.
4. Everyday B2B and item-level deals **do not stack with each other**. The customer gets the deeper of those two layers, not both.
5. Loyalty can still sit on the stack as shown in training. Do not volunteer loyalty mechanics unless asked.

The B2B promo column in event rooms is going away. Any leftover B2B *promo* input is **ignored** at go-live. If they only had B2B value inside a promo room and **no** Everyday B2B, they will not keep B2B pricing unless they add Everyday B2B.

### The one example you should memorize

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base cost | $100 |
| Everyday B2B | 5% → $95 on a non-promo day |
| Cyber B2C promo | 10% → B2C cost $90 |
| **After 11/9 B2B cost** | **$90 − 5% = $85.50** |

Say it out loud: the 5% is **off the promo cost**, not off $100. It is a $4.50 B2B increment, not $5.00. If you get this wrong, they will think you are hiding a point.

### The trap they will get wrong

**10% event + 5% Flash + 5% Everyday B2B ≠ 20%.**

Flash stacks on the event. Everyday B2B does **not** also stack on that Flash item. Pro customers do not get all three. On that SKU, the item-level deal wins if it is deeper than Everyday B2B, and B2C and B2B can land at the **same** price. When prices are not differentiated, the **Pro Price flag disappears**. That is expected, not a bug. Flash is excluded from Everyday B2B stacking on purpose so a short, high-value B2C push is not taxied by incremental Pro cost.

### Why we are doing this (supplier-safe version)

“We made it too hard to keep the B2B price you already intended. You had to re-enter the same discount in every room, and events were wiping Pro Pricing. Starting November 9, the Everyday B2B you set is the Everyday B2B Pro customers get on top of the live event cost. You set it once.”

Internal version (never say this to suppliers): if we land ~5% **effective** B2B discount, forecast is **$13M incremental B2B GRS** in Q4. Way Day 2025: SKUs that preserved B2B discounts during promos grew **+2,100 bps** vs those that did not. Everyday Pro Pricing investment: **+1,800 bps** B2B growth vs non-investors; **+1,200 bps** B2B vs B2C inside investors. Pro Price Filter is the #1 filter on the B2B site; **13%** of clickers convert in-session.

## 3. How to prepare before you speak

Open the data sheet. Find the supplier. Write four numbers on your one-pager before the call:

1. **Everyday B2B %** that will stack
2. **L90D effective B2B %** (the “stay margin-neutral” number)
3. **Bucket** (egregious / full erosion / partial / limited / none)
4. **Your ask** (keep current / move to effective / add Everyday B2B)

Column notes from training:

- Weighted catalog L90D effective when line-wide
- Separate view for items that actually have B2B discounts
- Negotiation Hub has part-level grain if they argue “that average is not my assortment”

**Ladder, in this order, every time**

1. Keep current Everyday B2B (ideal)
2. Move to L90D effective / stay investment-neutral
3. Land above 5% if they cannot hold current but can do better than effective
4. Floor at 5%, or 0% if they escalate
5. **Never** reduce B2C promo depth to “pay for” B2B

If they go to 0%, you lost the argument but you protected event health. Log it. Do not punish them in the same meeting by cutting Flash or holiday participation.

Perigold / SRB Trade Discount is separate. Stacking does not change the 10% retail Pro discount on those products. Do not mix “Trade program” and “Everyday B2B stacking” into one blob.

## 4. Role by supplier bucket

Prioritize **Egregious Risk**, then **Full Erosion**, then **Partial Erosion**. Limited erosion is optional. 0% B2B is FYI plus an add-discount pitch if the relationship can take it.

### Egregious risk (required)

**What it is:** Stacked result would be >20%, **or** the live discount would jump more than 10 points vs L90D effective. Training example pattern: ~23% Everyday vs ~3% effective.

**Why the call is required:** If they do not touch Pricing Home, November 9 can create unintentionally deep costs and **PO discrepancies**. This is a protection conversation as much as a growth conversation.

**Stance:** Calm, specific, on their numbers. You are preventing a surprise invoice, not accusing them of over-discounting.

**Ask:** Confirm they understand stacking. Recommend moving Everyday B2B toward L90D effective unless they *intend* the deep stack. Get a date they will update Pricing Home.

### Full erosion (required follow-up on Growth@)

They have Everyday B2B on paper and **0% effective during major promotions**. They already live in a world where events wipe Pro Pricing. Stacking turns the paper % into a real % overnight.

**Ask:** Keep Everyday B2B if they can. Otherwise move to L90D effective (what they have *actually* been giving). If they cannot commit, a lower % or 0% is allowed. B2C depth is not a funding source.

### Partial erosion (recommended follow-up)

Same pitch, slightly softer. They sometimes keep B2B through events, sometimes not. Stacking removes the need to babysit rooms.

### Limited erosion (optional)

They already manually protect B2B during promos. Message: this makes your life easier; come to the webinar; no action if you like current depth. Still confirm they *saw* Growth@. Silence is not awareness.

### No Everyday B2B

No cost change on 11/9. Pitch adding Everyday B2B *because maintenance is no longer the objection*. Do not pressure a 0% supplier the same week you are unwinding an egregious 23% supplier.

### Perigold sensitive (required list hygiene + different comms)

Labor Day erosion was severe (training bar: roughly >5% erosion / >75% of everyday wiped). Central team will **waive the 5% B2B minimum** and move them to L90D effective **unless they opt out by October 19**. Four Hands-style pattern: 10% Everyday, ~1% over Labor Day, ~6% L90D → auto-move to 6% with Growth@ confirmation.

**Your job:** Confirm they belong on the list. Remove false positives by 10/19. Tell them we are offering to do the admin so they stay investment-neutral after the earlier Trade Discount push. Do not surprise a design-trade brand with a 10% stacking on Cyber.

## 5. Call structure (20–25 minutes)

Use this in mocks and in real life.

**Minutes 0–3 — Frame, don’t defend**  
“I want to walk through a pricing change on November 9 so you are not finding it in Partner Home during Cyber. It is simpler, not a new ask for extra points.”

**Minutes 3–8 — Show the before/after on *their* SKU or a $100 example**  
One table. One sentence on Flash not double-stacking. Stop talking.

**Minutes 8–12 — Their numbers**  
“Your Everyday input is X. What Pro customers have actually been getting over 90 days is Y. On November 9, X will start landing during events unless we change the input.”

**Minutes 12–18 — Decision**  
Keep X / move to Y / something in between. Write down the number and the owner (them in Pricing Home vs, for PG sensitive, central move).

**Minutes 18–22 — Close the back doors**  
- Do not pull B2C to fund this  
- New parts still need Everyday B2B in PA 2.0  
- Flash still has the per-SKU fee; Flash items may not show Pro Price  
- Webinar Oct 13; you are available on the next call for anything their CFO sends back

**After the call:** Email a three-line recap: date of change, the number they chose, where to edit it. No new arguments.

## 6. Core talk tracks

### Opening (use almost verbatim)

“Starting November 9, Everyday B2B will apply on top of the live promotional cost instead of getting erased by it. You still control the percent in Pricing Home, and you can change it any time — it goes live immediately. I am not asking you to go deeper than you already planned. I am asking you to confirm the percent that is sitting there is the percent you actually want during Q4 events.”

### Why this is easier (for suppliers who already try to do the right thing)

“Today, keeping a 5% Pro price means submitting Everyday B2B, then rebuilding 5% incremental B2B in every event room, Flash, closeout, extended room, and aisle promo, then ending the aisle project by hand, then tagging new parts in PA 2.0. After November 9 it is: set Everyday B2B, and still add it on new parts. That is it.”

### Growth, without sounding like a quota

“Pro customers shop with the Pro Price filter first. When B2B pricing is real during events, those SKUs get the flag, the filter, and better conversion. When the event wipes the Pro gap, we lose that merchandising — and you still did the work of loading a discount that never showed up.”

Numbers you may use if they are data-oriented: +2,100 bps on SKUs that preserved B2B through Way Day 2025; Pro Price Filter #1 on site; 13% same-session conversion after the filter click. Do not cite the $13M Q4 forecast.

### Flash / item-level (say this before they invent 20%)

“Flash and Doorbusters will stack on the event. They will not also stack on Everyday B2B. If a SKU is on Flash, the Pro customer gets the Flash path, not Flash plus Pro. That is intentional so a short B2C push is not stacked three layers deep.”

### Net cost visibility

“In early October you will see base cost, which discounts are live or upcoming, whether they stack or deepest-win, and the final B2C and B2B wholesale cost — at the part and in catalog rollups. I would rather we look at that together than debate hypotheticals.”

## 7. Difficult conversations

Stay in this posture: **curious, specific, unmovable on B2C, flexible on Everyday B2B depth.**

### “This is a margin grab. You are forcing us 5 points deeper on Cyber.”

**Do not:** Argue intent. Do not say “it’s only $13M.” Do not say “everyone else is keeping 5%.”

**Do:** Separate *engine* from *input*.

“I get why it looks like that. Today your 10% Everyday is not what we invoice during events — effective has been closer to [Y]%. On November 9 the engine will finally honor the 10% unless we change the input. If your intent was never to give 10% during Cyber, we should set Everyday B2B to [Y]% so your investment matches history. That is margin-neutral. What I cannot do is leave 10% in the tool and also promise it will keep getting wiped.”

**If they need a face-saving win:** Offer to do the Pricing Home walk-through on the call so they are not “giving” something; they are correcting a stale setting.

### “We will just take 5 points out of B2C to pay for B2B.”

This is the red line. Firm, not dramatic.

“I need to be direct on that one. Pulling B2C to fund B2B is not an option we can support. B2C events still have to clear merchandising and compliance on their own. B2B is an incremental Pro layer. If the stacked B2B cost is too rich, we change Everyday B2B — keep current, move to your effective rate, or go lower. We do not thin holiday or Flash depth to make the Pro math work.”

If they insist, pause the B2B decision, protect the B2C submission, and escalate to your lead the same day. Do not “think about it” overnight; that is how event rooms come in light.

### “Fine. We will set Everyday B2B to zero.”

Treat it as a loss of Pro presence, not a personal failure. Then put consequences on the table without threatening.

“You can do that, and it takes effect immediately. I want you to know what you are turning off: Pro Price flag and filter eligibility on those SKUs, the conversion those flags drive, and a simpler stack we cannot reverse in a source room during Cyber. If zero is a protest and not a P&L decision, can we park at [effective %] through Q4 and revisit in January once you have seen net cost in Partner Home?”

If they still go to zero: thank them, log it, do **not** retaliate on B2C. Revisit after they see a Cyber week without Pro merchandising.

### “We already give you trade / we already bleed on Wayfair.”

Especially common on Perigold-cross and design-forward Tex Dex brands.

“Trade Discount on PG/SRB is a different program and is not changing. This conversation is only about whether Everyday B2B in Pricing Home should stay at [X] or move to [Y] so stacking does not accidentally double the investment you already feel. I am trying to keep you whole, not stack a new ask on Trade.”

### “Our CFO / VP will never approve a deeper Cyber cost.”

Give them a packet, not a speech.

“I will send three things they can react to: (1) go-live is November 9, backend only; (2) $100 → 10% event = $90 B2C → 5% B2B = $85.50; (3) recommended input [Y]% to stay at your last-90-day effective investment. They do not have to approve going deeper. They have to approve that the number in Pricing Home matches intent.”

Offer a 15-minute three-way. Do not let the SRM-to-AE relationship absorb a CFO no that was never briefed.

### “Nobody told us. This is last minute.”

“Fair. The site change is November 9, not this week. Growth@ is the formal notice, the webinar is October 13, and Partner Home visibility lands in early October so you can see it before it bills. I am on this call so you can decide the input with time to spare — including leaving it alone.”

Then book the follow-up. “Happy to chat through these changes in our next call” only works if there *is* a next call on the calendar.

### “Show me SKU-level. Your 6% average is wrong.”

Agree. Averages hide heroes.

“Let’s open Negotiation Hub at part level. If 80% of GRS is already at 3% and a tail is at 15%, we should not ship a line-wide 12% into stacking. We can set line-wide to the honest core and handle exceptions.”

Do not die on the catalog average if their mix is obviously barbelled. Getting a clean core % is the win.

### “What about Flash this week / doorbusters / DOTD?”

“Item-level deals become incremental to the live B2C promo, which is why you submit them. They still do not replace a real event cost — other B2C rooms stay deepest-wins. They still do not stack with Everyday B2B. We expect more SKUs to clear Flash rules; the per-SKU Flash fee still applies.”

If they ask whether DOTD is in: official CM wrap-up includes Flash, DOTD, and Doorbusters. Jenny’s item-level slide called out Flash and Doorbusters specifically. If a supplier is DOTD-heavy, confirm in `#temp-stacking-questions-26` before you promise DOTD stacking.

### “Will Pro Price still show during Cyber?”

“When Everyday B2B creates a lower Pro cost than B2C, yes. If a Flash Deal matches or beats Everyday B2B, B2C and B2B can be the same price and the flag will not show on that SKU. Flag = differentiated Pro price, not ‘this item exists on the Pro site.’”

### “We load B2B in the promo spreadsheet. We’ll keep doing that.”

“That column is going away. On November 9 the system ignores B2B promo inputs. If you want Pro pricing, it has to live as Everyday B2B. Promo-room B2B will not save you.”

UK / Cyber transition nuance if they are live in a Cyber room on 11/8–11/9: rooms that were using B2B promo cost on 11/8 switch to stacked B2C promo + Everyday B2B on 11/9 so we do **not** double-count. Do not freelance this. If they have an active Cyber row, check Slack.

### “Our Everyday is 12% but we always intended it to erode. That’s the deal.”

“Then the honest input is closer to [effective]%, and stacking is how we stop playing hide-and-seek with rooms. I would rather lock [Y]% that actually invoices than keep 12% as theater.”

This is often the real Tex Dex conversation — decorative brands who used a high Everyday as a “Pro story” knowing events would crush it.

### “We’ll wait and see after Cyber.”

“The engine will not wait. Whatever is in Pricing Home on November 9 is what stacks. Waiting is a decision to keep [X]%. If that is not intended, we should change it this month.”

### “Can you just do it for us?”

- **Perigold sensitive:** Yes, via the central process, unless they opt out by 10/19.
- **Everyone else:** They (or you, if you already have Pricing Home access as part of normal SRM support) update Everyday B2B. Do not promise a central move that does not exist.

### They go silent after Growth@

Do not assume they read it. Send the short email, then a calendar hold titled “15 min: November 9 discount change.” High-bucket suppliers get a phone call if they no-show.

### They escalate to your manager or a buyer

Brief your lead first with: bucket, X vs Y, what you asked, what they offered, whether B2C is in play. Same ladder. Do not reopen B2C as a bargaining chip upstairs.

### Rebates

“Manual B2B rebates already stack and are unchanged. Do not pull rebate and Everyday into one negotiation unless they bring it up.”

## 8. Tex Dex–specific posture

Tex Dex books mix high Everyday decorative brands, event-heavy seasonal, and suppliers who live in Flash. That produces three failure modes:

1. **Pretty Everyday, ugly effective** — rugs-adjacent / décor analog to Compamia (12% paper, ~6% real). Pitch: keep 12% if they want Pro to be real in Q4; otherwise lock 6% and stop the room gymnastics.
2. **Flash-native assortments** — they will obsess over triple-stack. Front-load the trap. Protect Flash participation; do not let stacking anxiety kill short-term B2C.
3. **“We’ll fix it in the extended room”** — extended / superrooms still do **not** stack with event rooms. There is no workaround of parking B2B in an extended room. Everyday B2B is the only durable Pro layer.

When you choose a mock-negotiation supplier, pick one of those three, not the partner who already loves Pro Pricing.
